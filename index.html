Editorial Standards

Peer Review Policy

Browse our comprehensive evaluation framework and guidelines below.

1. Overview of the Review Process

Pinnacle Academic Press is committed to maintaining the highest standards of academic integrity and scholarly excellence. All research articles submitted to this journal undergo a rigorous Double-Blind Peer-Review process.

Under this system:

  • The reviewers do not know the identity of the authors.
  • The authors do not know the identity of the reviewers.

This ensures an objective, unbiased, and fair evaluation of all manuscripts, free from institutional or personal prejudices.

2. Technical Screening (Initial Review)

Upon submission, the Managing Editor conducts a preliminary technical evaluation. Manuscripts may be rejected immediately at this stage if they:

  • Fall outside the scope or aims of the journal.
  • Do not follow the mandated formatting standards (strictly utilizing the APA 7th Edition structure and an IMRAD format).
  • Exhibit a similarity index that exceeds the journal's strict plagiarism threshold via verification software (e.g., Turnitin).
  • Lack clear institutional ethical clearances or feature unresolved conflicts of interest.

Manuscripts that clear the initial screening are assigned to an Editor, who forwards the anonymized file to a minimum of two (2) independent external experts in the matching field of study.

3. Policy on AI-Assisted Research and Writing

To preserve academic authorship, transparency, and accountability, Pinnacle Academic Press enforces strict guidelines regarding Artificial Intelligence (AI):

  • Authorship Restrictions: AI tools (such as Large Language Models or automated writing assistants) cannot be listed as an author or co-author under any circumstances.
  • Permitted Usage: Authors may utilize AI tools solely to improve the readability, language, grammar, or phrasing of their original manuscript.
  • Strict Prohibitions: AI tools must not be used to generate core research findings, interpret study data, design methodologies, or synthesize literature reviews.
  • Mandatory Disclosure: If AI tools were utilized during the manuscript's preparation, the authors must explicitly declare this in a dedicated "Declaration of AI Use" section before the References. Authors retain full accountability for the accuracy and originality of all content within their paper.

4. Reviewer Evaluation Criteria

External reviewers evaluate the manuscript based on intellectual merit, methodological soundness, and contribution to the discipline. Reviewers are instructed to assess:

  • Originality and Relevance: Does the paper introduce novel insights or frameworks to the existing literature?
  • Methodological Rigor: Is the research design appropriate? Are the qualitative, quantitative, or mixed-method data frameworks correctly applied and robust?
  • Ethical Compliance: Are human participant protections, institutional approvals, and ethical boundaries explicitly addressed?
  • Clarity and Coherence: Is the manuscript structured logically, with clear transitions between the introduction, methodology, data presentation, and discussion.

5. Editorial Decisions

Based on the detailed feedback and recommendations provided by the peer reviewers, the Editor makes one of the following decisions:

  • Accept As Is: The manuscript satisfies all criteria and is accepted for publication without any adjustments.
  • Accept with Minor Revisions: The paper is accepted in principle, pending minor modifications, formatting fixes, or minor textual clarifications.
  • Major Revisions (Revise and Resubmit): The paper requires substantial restructuring, deep theoretical revisions, or additional data verification. The revised manuscript will undergo a second round of evaluation.
  • Reject: The paper contains critical methodological flaws, fails to show incremental value, or fails to meet the journal's rigorous scientific standards.

The final decision rests entirely with the Editorial Board. Authors receive comprehensive, constructive, and confidential feedback reports to help improve their work.

6. Timeline and Accountability

The journal values efficiency and strives to complete the initial round of peer review within 4 to 6 weeks from submission. Reviewers are expected to honor agreed deadlines and maintain absolute confidentiality regarding the manuscript's contents throughout the entire evaluation cycle.
